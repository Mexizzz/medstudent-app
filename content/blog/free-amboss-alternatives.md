AMBOSS is a genuinely excellent resource — a searchable medical library plus a question bank in one. But it's a paid subscription, and not every student can justify the cost on top of UWorld, Anki add-ons, and everything else. If you're looking for a **free AMBOSS alternative**, here are five tools medical students actually use, and where each one fits.

## 1. MedStudy — free AI question generation from your own notes

If what you value most in AMBOSS is the **question bank**, MedStudy covers that need differently: instead of a fixed set of questions, it generates MCQs, flashcards, and clinical cases from *your* uploaded notes or textbook chapters — for free.

**Best for:** turning your actual lectures into unlimited practice questions, drilling weak topics, and studying on your specific curriculum.

**Why it's a strong free option:** there's a genuinely usable free tier, an AI tutor that explains your wrong answers, and no cap on generating more questions for a topic you keep missing. It won't replace AMBOSS's curated medical library, but for active recall on your own material it's hard to beat at the price. [Try it free here.](https://www.medstudy.space)

## 2. Anki — free spaced repetition

The classic. Anki's spaced-repetition algorithm is one of the most effective learning tools ever built, and it's free on desktop and Android (the iOS app is paid). Community decks like AnKing cover huge swathes of the curriculum.

**Best for:** long-term retention of facts.

**Trade-off:** making your own cards is slow, and pre-made decks are generic rather than tailored to your lectures. Many students pair Anki with an [AI tool that generates cards from their notes](/blog/ai-flashcard-generator-medical-students) to cut the card-making time.

## 3. Free trial Qbanks + question-of-the-day

Most major Qbanks (including AMBOSS itself) offer free trials or a free daily question. Stacking these gives you a rotating supply of high-quality exam-style questions at no cost.

**Best for:** a steady drip of well-written questions.

**Trade-off:** limited volume, and you can't focus them on your specific weak areas.

## 4. Open educational resources

There's a huge amount of free, high-quality medical education online — lecture series, open-access notes, and reference sites. Paired with a way to test yourself, these cover most of the "medical library" role that AMBOSS plays.

**Best for:** content and reference on a budget.

**Trade-off:** quality varies, and reading alone isn't retrieval — you still need to test yourself on what you read. (Here's [why active recall matters so much](/blog/how-to-pass-usmle-step-1).)

## 5. Your own notes + an AI tutor

Your lecture notes are already tailored to exactly what you'll be examined on — they're just usually passive. Feed them into an AI study tool and they become active: questions to answer, cases to work through, and a tutor to explain gaps.

**Best for:** studying precisely what your course covers, for free.

## Which Free Alternative Should You Pick?

- **Want AMBOSS's question bank on a budget?** MedStudy — free AI-generated questions from your own material.
- **Want long-term retention?** Anki, ideally with AI-generated cards to save time.
- **Want more high-quality practice questions?** Stack free-trial Qbanks and daily questions.
- **Want AMBOSS's library role?** Open educational resources plus a way to test yourself.

The honest answer for most students is a **combination**: free spaced repetition for retention, plus an AI question generator to turn your own notes into unlimited practice. That covers the two things AMBOSS does — library and Qbank — without the subscription.
