The UK Medical Licensing Assessment (UKMLA) is now the common standard every doctor must pass to practise in the UK, whether you're a UK student or an international medical graduate on the PLAB route. If you're revising for it, here's a clear, practical guide to what it involves and how to prepare efficiently.

## What the UKMLA Actually Is

The UKMLA has two components:

- **The Applied Knowledge Test (AKT)** — a computer-based, single-best-answer exam testing clinical knowledge across the curriculum.
- **The Clinical and Professional Skills Assessment (CPSA)** — a practical, station-based assessment of clinical and communication skills, run by your medical school (or via PLAB 2 for IMGs).

Most of your written revision effort goes into the **AKT**, so that's where an efficient strategy matters most.

## What to Prioritise

The UKMLA content map is broad, but it's weighted toward **common and important presentations** — the bread-and-butter of UK practice. That means:

- High-yield areas: acute presentations, common chronic disease management, prescribing safety, and the topics that appear again and again in clinical practice.
- The **"Acute and emergency"** presentations and safe prescribing are disproportionately important — don't leave them late.
- Professional, ethical, and legal principles relevant to UK practice (consent, capacity, safeguarding, confidentiality).

Rather than trying to cover everything equally, revise in proportion to how common and how dangerous a topic is.

## The Efficient Way to Revise: Test Yourself

The trap in UKMLA revision is spending weeks passively reading guidelines and lecture notes. The evidence — and the experience of students who do well — points the other way: **active recall and spaced repetition** are what actually make knowledge stick under exam pressure.

A practical loop:

1. **Learn a topic** from your notes or a trusted resource.
2. **Immediately test yourself** on it with single-best-answer questions.
3. **Review what you got wrong** and schedule it to come back with spaced repetition.
4. **Repeat** across the content map, prioritising common presentations.

The faster you can turn a revision topic into questions, the more of your time becomes retrieval rather than re-reading.

## A Simple Revision Timeline

- **8+ weeks out:** Work through the content map topic by topic, testing yourself as you go. Build your spaced-repetition backlog.
- **4 weeks out:** Shift to mixed, timed question practice to build exam pace and stamina. Target weak areas your practice flags.
- **Final week:** Light review of high-yield and previously-missed topics. Prioritise prescribing safety and acute presentations. Rest properly.

## For CPSA / Clinical Skills

The CPSA is practical — practise histories, examinations, and communication with peers under timed conditions. Knowledge from your AKT revision directly supports your clinical reasoning in stations, so the two reinforce each other.

## Bottom Line

The UKMLA rewards **broad, prioritised knowledge tested actively** — not passive cramming. Revise common and dangerous presentations first, convert your reading into question practice as early as possible, and use spaced repetition so nothing decays before exam day.
